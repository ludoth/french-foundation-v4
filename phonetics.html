🗣️ Pronunciation Guide

How to read the phonetics in this app

📖
What system does this app use?

Each French word shows a respelling in grey brackets underneath — for example bonjour → [bohn-ZHOOR]. It's written for English readers: just say it out loud as if it were English.

This is not IPA (the dictionary symbols like /bɔ̃.ʒuʁ/). The goal is practical pronunciation you can read instantly — close enough to be understood, then perfected by listening.

The STRESSED syllable is in CAPITALS. In French the stress almost always lands on the last syllable, so most words end loud: [mair-SEE], [a-ree-VAY].

🎉
Good news: no tones

Unlike many languages, French has no tones — pitch doesn't change a word's meaning. The three things that actually trip up English speakers are:

Nasal vowelsohnspoken through the nose
The French uew"ee" with rounded lips
Silent letters·final consonants often dropped
💡 Tap the 🔊 Listen button on any card to hear the French read aloud — the fastest way to get these right.

Nasal vowels — the French signature

These have no English equivalent. Let the sound resonate through your nose and don't fully pronounce the n/m.

🎨
Gender colour-coding (new!)

Every French noun is masculine or feminine, and the app shows it two ways so it sinks in: the article (le / la) is part of the word, and the word is colour-coded with a small tag.

le père (m)Masculine · blue
la mère (f)Feminine · rose
l'ami (m/f)Either · violet

Learn the noun with its article from day one — "la table", never just "table". It's the single best habit for getting gender right later.

💡 Words with no colour or tag are not gendered nouns — verbs (manger), adjectives (grand), and phrases. Adjectives list their feminine form in the card note (e.g. "f: grande").
